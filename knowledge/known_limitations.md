# Audio QA limits

- A second-level mark can identify a search window and can miss the corresponding word by several seconds. The rough-point benchmark measures temporal overlap. Use reviewed intervals and reviewed unmatched candidates for precision, F1, false positives per hour and boundary error.
- The first recording has a compressed stereo master and approximate labels. The source script, original photograph, provider metadata and native decisions were unavailable during the study. An MP3 measurement alone cannot identify the provider's synthesis mechanism.
- A short broadband event can be a natural release, fricative or breath. F0 tracking can fail at consonants and rapid voicing changes. Whole-window harmonicity and formants require phonetic context. Repetition autocorrelation remains a proxy.
- Whisper predicts written words and can normalize unlike pronunciations to the same spelling. Wav2vec2 predicts a particular phone inventory with an uncalibrated CTC model. Its nonblank spans are frame activity. Context-specific vowel and consonant lengths, realized tonal accent and exact phone boundaries remain open without appropriate alignment and native comparison.
- Clip-level AudioSet scores and audio-language model responses are supplementary. Language switches, speaker changes and compressor effects can raise nearest-neighbor distance. An unverified same-track control can contain a defect.
- A trial may reduce energy while damaging a natural phone. This study has no accepted real-audio repair. The original is the reference for all auditions.
- The 95% recall and 80–90% review-reduction targets require independent records with adjudicated examples. No classifier was trained on this single lecture.
